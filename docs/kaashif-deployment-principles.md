# Kaashif's deployment principles

These are the default principles for deploying Kaashif's Brighton Take and Hold Necron list. Apply them to each mission layout unless the terrain or deployment zone makes one impossible; any exception should be called out in that layout's plan.

## Decision order

Build each deployment from the actual pairing, not from the map alone:

1. Read the opponent's complete published list and identify pre-game moves, Infiltrators, fast melee launch pieces, long-range shooting, indirect fire, deep strike, and large vehicle or monster threats. Never infer an absent rule merely from a faction name; if the list is missing or unusable, label the review as list-unavailable.
2. Read Kaashif's primary objective for that force-disposition matchup. Decide whether the plan must retain a home presence or can **Abandon Home** and commit both Flayed One units forward.
3. Select the correct A, B, or C battlefield layout and confirm the exact deployment-zone geometry before placing a base.
4. Apply the unit principles below, then the opponent and objective overrides. A specific matchup override wins over the generic default.
5. Record the opponent, objective, layout, applied overrides, reserves, and any terrain compromise beside the finished deployment.

## Unit roles and placement

- **Wraiths:** deploy both units as far forward as practical while keeping legal coherency. When a natural-objective ruin can fully screen one brick, put that brick completely behind and off the footprint so it cannot be shot from the opposing deployment zone; use terrain contact for the other brick where appropriate.
- **Missile group:** treat the Nightbringer, Skorpekh Destroyers, Skorpekh Lord, and Ammentar as one compact forward group rather than four independent pieces.
- **Skorpekh Destroyers:** deploy as far forward as practical behind the right-side terrain, arranged for a clean move out. Toe the footprint when legal, but if the deployment boundary leaves a physical gap, prioritize being completely screened over claiming cover.
- **Skorpekh Lord:** deploy attached to and in coherency with the Skorpekh Destroyers, inside the same missile group.
- **Ammentar (Nekrosor):** deploy inside the missile group and close enough to the Skorpekh unit to benefit from Lone Operative while supporting their advance.
- **Nightbringer:** deploy as far forward as its full 90mm base can legally fit behind the right-side terrain and beside the Skorpekhs and Ammentar. It must have zero overlap with every terrain footprint and no sight line from the opposing deployment zone; use a small clearance buffer rather than merely placing its centre off terrain.
- **Canoptek Reanimator:** deploy forward behind a left-side square or ruin so it can advance behind a Wraith brick. Do not toe into the terrain footprint, and require it to be screened from the opposing deployment zone.
- **Flayed Ones, forward unit:** infiltrate one unit far forward, usually near the centre and behind a wall. Keep the unit off the terrain footprint so it is hidden rather than merely receiving cover.
- **Flayed Ones, second unit:** use the other unit as a rear or side screen, adjusted to the layout and opposing reserves.
- **Scout/fast-melee override:** against a fast melee army with Scout moves, put a Flayed One unit directly in front of each important launch lane. The aim is to constrain the legal Scout move and force the first melee wave to spend movement clearing a cheap screen. Do not leave a convenient consolidation path into the Nightbringer, Skorpekhs, Reanimator, or Wraiths. If the objective is **Abandon Home**, use both Flayed One units as forward move-blockers rather than retaining the second unit as a home or reserve screen.
- **Fast melee without Scout:** keep the forward Flayed Ones far enough ahead to absorb or redirect the first charge, but do not donate them if their death would create a direct pile-in or consolidation route into the missile group.
- **Long-range or high-volume shooting:** treat any ray from the opposing deployment zone to the Nightbringer, Reanimator, or missile group as a failed deployment. Prefer full obscuration over a slightly shorter first move.
- **Deep strike and reserve pressure:** retain the second Flayed One unit as a rear or side screen unless the objective says Abandon Home or the Scout/fast-melee override needs both units forward.
- **Abandon Home objective:** no unit is assigned to sit on the home objective. Both Flayed One units deploy forward, the Wraiths form the first wave, and the missile group follows compactly; the exact order still has to deny enemy Scout and charge lanes.
- **C'tan Shard of the Void Dragon:** begin in Strategic Reserves/deep strike.

## Plan checks

Before accepting a deployment:

1. Confirm every model is wholly inside the deployment zone except legal Infiltrators and reserves.
2. Confirm the screened Wraith brick is wholly behind and off its terrain; check terrain contact for the other Wraith brick.
3. Confirm the Nightbringer's complete 90mm base, with a small safety buffer, has no terrain-footprint overlap. Confirm the Reanimator and forward Flayed Ones also have no terrain-footprint overlap.
4. Confirm the Nightbringer, Skorpekhs, Skorpekh Lord, and Ammentar form one compact forward missile group behind the right-side terrain.
5. Audit the complete bases, not just their centres, from a grid of firing points across the entire opposing deployment zone. Any clear ray to a unit intended to be hidden is a failed plan.
6. Confirm attached Leaders are in coherency with their units and Ammentar is close enough to the Skorpekhs for the intended Lone Operative protection.
7. Confirm the forward units have unobstructed first moves and legal base clearance around walls.
8. Confirm the screening unit covers a useful reserve-entry or deep-strike lane.
9. Check the opposing list for Scout moves and fast melee before locking the Flayed One positions; apply the two-unit Abandon Home override when relevant.
10. Check that a sacrificial Flayed One screen cannot be used as a pile-in or consolidation bridge into a valuable unit.
11. Save the review with a stable numbered ID so revisions and comments can refer to one deployment unambiguously.

These principles describe the intended starting position, not a fixed first-turn script. Opponent threat ranges, redeploys, and who has first turn can still change the final placement.
